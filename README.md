> **How this newspaper works.** You cannot write directly into this paper; only the editors can. So you *fork* it (get your own printing press), *clone* it (bring the press home), write your story, run the proofreader (`python3 scripts/proofread.py`), *push* to your fork, and *open a pull request*. The editor reads it, the robot proofreader checks it, and if both are happy, your words go into tomorrow's edition. Full instructions live in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

### CITY COUNCIL VOTES TO REPLACE ALL TRAFFIC LIGHTS WITH VERY POLITE VOLUNTEERS

*By Priya Raman, Civic Affairs Desk*

In a decision that surprised everyone except the volunteers themselves, the City Council voted 7–2 on Saturday night to retire the last of the city's 1,214 traffic lights by the end of the year. In their place will stand members of the newly formed Courtesy Corps, a group of 3,000 residents who have pledged to wave, nod, and say "after you" at every major intersection.

"We ran the numbers," said Council President Dana Okafor, holding up a spreadsheet nobody could read from the gallery. "A traffic light costs eleven thousand a year to maintain. A volunteer costs a cup of tea and a raincoat."

Critics were quick to point out that the pilot programme, run over three weeks in the Old Market district, produced what one engineer described as "the world's first traffic jam caused entirely by people insisting the other person go first." The council has responded by adding a Rule of Three: after three rounds of "no, after you," the person on the left must proceed.

The Courtesy Corps begins training on Monday. Uniforms, we are told, will be a tasteful orange.

### LOCAL BAKERY'S SOURDOUGH STARTER TURNS 100, RECEIVES KEY TO THE CITY

*By Tomasz Wierzbicki, Food & Drink*

Bubbles, a sourdough starter kept alive by four generations of the Almeida family, celebrated its centenary on Saturday with a small ceremony at Almeida & Daughters on Kettle Street. The mayor presented the jar with a ceremonial key, which was then placed on a shelf where, the family admits, it will be ignored in favour of the flour.

"My great-grandmother started it in 1926 with rye flour and rainwater," said Inês Almeida, 34, who now runs the shop. "It has survived two floods, one fire, a cat, and my cousin Rui, who once tried to feed it soda."

Regulars queued around the block for the anniversary loaf, a 100-hour cold-fermented boule that sold out by 8:15 a.m. Bubbles was unavailable for comment but was described by staff as "lively."

## 🌍 WORLD

### INTERNATIONAL COMMITTEE AGREES ON THE SHAPE OF A STANDARD SPOON

*By Leila Haddad, Foreign Correspondent*

After eleven years of negotiation, delegates from 63 countries emerged from a conference hall in Geneva late on Saturday to announce that they had finally agreed on the dimensions of a standard teaspoon. The new International Reference Spoon measures 5.0 millilitres, as most people assumed it already did.

"The hard part was never the volume," said the committee's chair, Dr. Annelie Berg. "The hard part was the handle. One delegation wanted it longer. Another wanted it flat. We had a two-year deadlock over whether it should be allowed to have a small decorative flower."

The flower, in the end, is optional.

The spoon will be cast in a special alloy and stored in a vault beside the reference kilogram, which reportedly asked to be moved to a quieter shelf.

### ISLAND NATION ANNOUNCES IT WILL SIMPLY MOVE SLIGHTLY TO THE LEFT

*By Kofi Mensah, Pacific Bureau*

The government of Talo, a nation of 4,200 people across three coral atolls, has announced a plan to relocate the entire country roughly 400 metres to the west. Officials say the new position enjoys better afternoon light and is, in the words of the prime minister, "a bit more out of the way of that one storm."

Engineers were initially sceptical. They remain sceptical. But they have also noticed that the plan has a surprisingly detailed budget, a timeline, and a very confident architect named Sela Fanua who once moved a lighthouse using three trucks and "a great deal of rope."

Talo's neighbours have wished it well and asked that it please not bump into them.

## 💻 TECHNOLOGY

### DEVELOPER ACCIDENTALLY PUSHES TO MAIN, IS IMMEDIATELY PROMOTED TO MAINTAINER

*By Ananya Bose, Technology Editor*

A junior developer at the software company Fernwood Labs became an overnight legend on Saturday after accidentally pushing a change directly to the main branch of the company's flagship product. The change, a single-line fix to a date formatting bug, was perfect. Nobody knows how.

"We have branch protection. We have required reviews. We have two bots whose entire job is to stop this," said engineering manager Hugo Lindqvist. "Somehow all three were asleep at the same moment. It's like a solar eclipse for CI."

The developer, who asked to be identified only as "R.," has since been given commit rights to the main repository, a small trophy, and a strongly worded pamphlet on the importance of forks and pull requests.

"I've read the pamphlet," R. said. "I've read it twice. I would like everyone to know I am now a big fan of pull requests."

### NEW KEYBOARD SHIPS WITH A DEDICATED "UNDO THAT, PLEASE" KEY

*By Ananya Bose, Technology Editor*

Peripheral maker Klack has unveiled the K-7, a mechanical keyboard whose most talked-about feature is a large, satisfying, cherry-red key in the top-right corner labelled simply **UNDO THAT, PLEASE**. It sends Ctrl+Z, but Klack insists it sends it "with feeling."

Early reviews are positive. One tester reported pressing it 400 times in an afternoon, "mostly out of habit, partly out of joy." The K-7 also includes a smaller key labelled *no, really, I meant it*, which sends Ctrl+Y.

### GIT COMMIT MESSAGES DISCOVERED TO BE 60 PERCENT "FIX," STUDY FINDS

*By Marcus Tan, Data Desk*

A survey of 12 million public commit messages published on Saturday by the Institute of Version Control Studies found that the single most common commit message is `fix`, followed by `fix again`, `wip`, `final`, and `final final`. Researchers found that commits labelled `final` were followed by a further commit an average of 4.3 times.

The study's lead author, Dr. Yumi Sato, said the results were "not surprising, but somehow still disappointing," and recommended that developers "write the message you'd want to read at 2 a.m. when everything is on fire."

## 🧪 SCIENCE

### ASTRONOMERS CONFIRM NEW MOON IS "JUST A ROCK, BUT A NICE ONE"

*By Elena Vasquez, Science Correspondent*

The Lowell Observatory has confirmed the discovery of a small moon orbiting the asteroid 2019 QX-7. The moon, provisionally named Pebble, is 38 metres across and, according to lead astronomer Dr. Farid Nasser, "not much to look at, but we've grown quite fond of it."

Pebble completes an orbit every 14 hours and has already been assigned a fan account on social media with 22,000 followers, most of whom, the observatory notes, are the observatory.

### BOTANISTS ANNOUNCE THE HOUSEPLANT THAT CANNOT BE KILLED; PUBLIC ACCEPTS CHALLENGE

*By Elena Vasquez, Science Correspondent*

After six years of breeding, researchers at the Greenhaven Botanical Institute have released *Sansevieria invicta*, a snake plant they describe as "indifferent to neglect, sarcasm, and central heating." The plant survived the institute's full torture protocol: nine weeks without water, a fortnight in a cupboard, and a week on the desk of a graduate student "who has previously killed a cactus."

Within hours of the announcement, the institute received 3,800 emails from members of the public volunteering to try.

### COFFEE PRICES FALL AFTER SUPPLIER ADMITS IT WAS "JUST SEEING WHAT WE COULD GET AWAY WITH"

*By Samuel Adeyemi, Markets*

Wholesale coffee prices dropped 18 percent on Saturday after Brightbean Trading, one of the region's largest importers, issued a candid statement acknowledging that the previous three price increases "were not, strictly speaking, based on anything."

"We raised it, and people kept buying it," read the statement. "Then we raised it again. At some point somebody in the office asked why, and nobody had an answer, so here we are."

Shares in Brightbean fell 6 percent on the news, then rose 9 percent after analysts decided honesty was "a growth sector."

## 🎭 ARTS & CULTURE

### MUSEUM UNVEILS EXHIBITION OF THINGS PEOPLE LEFT IN THE LOST AND FOUND

*By Grace Nwosu, Arts Editor*

The Municipal Museum's new show, *Unclaimed*, opens today with 412 objects retrieved from the city's lost and found offices over the past decade. Highlights include a single roller skate, a trombone, 71 umbrellas arranged by colour, a jar labelled "DO NOT OPEN" (unopened), and a handwritten novel of 300 pages whose author has never come forward.

"We think of it as a portrait of the city," said curator Ines Moreau. "Everyone here lost something. Then they kept going."

The exhibition runs until November. Admission is free. Please keep hold of your belongings.

## 🗣️ OPINION

### WHY YOU SHOULD ALWAYS READ THE PAMPHLET

*Editorial*

This week a young developer at Fernwood Labs pushed straight to main and got lucky. We wish them well. But luck is not a workflow.

The pull request exists for a simple reason: nobody, however brilliant, is right every time. A fork gives you a safe place to try. A clone gives you a place to test. A push shares your work without forcing it on anyone. A pull request asks, politely, "would you take a look?" It is a small act of humility built into a tool, and it is the reason strangers on the internet can build things together without setting them on fire.

Read the pamphlet. Then open a pull request.
